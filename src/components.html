Shaping Qatar’s Business Landscape

The Qatar Financial Centre (QFC) is a leading onshore business and financial platform, enabling firms to operate seamlessly within Qatar’s dynamic market. Offering a unique combination of flexibility, international standards and strategic access, QFC empowers businesses to scale, innovate and thrive in one of the region’s most stable economies.Established under Law No. 7 of 2005 by the State of Qatar, QFC is a core pillar of Qatar’s diversification agenda under Qatar National Vision 2030. Sports

Qatar’s sports investments have transformed the country’s global reputation and positioned the country as a key player in the international sports business landscape, aligned with the goals outlined in the Qatar National Vision 2030.

Following the success of global events such as the FIFA World Cup 2022™, Qatar has continued to expand its $20 billion+ sports economy. The nation now hosts over 80 international sporting events annually, including landmark occasions like the Formula 1 Qatar Grand Prix 2025, the 2030 Asian Games and a bid to host the 2036 Olympics and Paralympic Games.

Each event attracts a wealth of allied sub-sectors and industries, from facilities management to sports education, sports medicine, tourism, data and analytics, and merchandising, turning billions in profit and underpinning Qatar’s position as an attractive business destination for global sports companies.

The Qatar Financial Centre (QFC) plays a central role in enabling this growth. Through initiatives like the QFC Sports Accelerator, developed in collaboration with the Aspire Zone Foundation and its partnership with Qatar Development Bank (QDB) to support Qatar SportsTech, QFC provides a platform for both local and international sports-focused startups and enterprises.

QFC provides direct access to Qatar's sports economy, a rapidly growing industry brimming with innovation, commercial opportunity, and regional reach, for foreign businesses wishing to enter the MENA market.

QFC provides direct access for international firms wanting to enter Qatar’s rapidly expanding sports sector and gain access to the wider region.

Digital & Media

Qatar’s fast-growing digital and media sector continues to unlock new opportunities for collaboration across ministries, government agencies and private enterprises. As a regional leader in digital adoption, the country is attracting global interest from companies looking to explore its connected and innovation-driven market.

As Qatar pushes forward with its ambitious development goals, it aims to generate QAR 40 billion in digital sector GDP impact by 2030 and create 26,000 new jobs, strengthening its role as a global leader in the digital economy.

Key areas driving this momentum include:

  • Cloud computing
  • Cybersecurity
  • Artificial Intelligence (AI)
  • Blockchain
  • Internet of Things (IoT)
  • AR/VR, e-commerce, and data analytics

QFC offers competitive benefits and incentives to digital service firms looking to set up businesses or expand their operations in Qatar, including initiatives such as the QFC Tech Circle, a shared working space for eligible tech firms and the knowledge-exchange platform QFC Tech Talk Series.

Inside Qatar

Qatar Deepens Global Economic and Strategic Ties

Over the past two months, Qatar has expanded bilateral trade and investment ties across multiple regions. New agreements and delegations advanced cooperation with Turkey, Sweden, Hong Kong, the U.S., and Malaysia, with the latter seeing trade volume rise of nearly 80%. The Qatar-Hong Kong merchandise trade corridor alone topped $1.6 billion last year. Qatar’s $500 billion U.S. investment push by its sovereign wealth fund also underscores its growing international economic footprint.

The Ministry of Commerce and Industry announced plans to launch 38 new e-services in 2025, signaling an ambitious digital transformation. In a tech-forward move, Qatar also partnered with Microsoft to introduce AI-powered virtual assistants for improved citizen services.

Steady Economic Performance and Investor Momentum

Qatar recorded $2.7 billion in FDI for 2024, driven by resilient non-oil sectors. Oxford Economics projects steady non-energy growth through 2025, with a sharper pickup expected in 2026. Complementing this, Invest Qatar unveiled $1 billion in incentives, reinforcing the country’s competitiveness, which was recognized in the 2025 Global Competitiveness Report, where Qatar ranked among the global leaders for the first time.

Yousuf Mohamed Al-Jaida

Chairman | Minister of Commerce and Industry

Yousuf Mohamed Al-Jaida was appointed Chief Executive Officer of Qatar Financial Centre (QFC) in June 2015. He previously held the role of Deputy Chief Executive Officer and Chief Strategic Business Development Officer at QFC where he was responsible for leading the overall strategy and business development functions of the organisation

Prior to joining QFC, he was Head of Indirect Investment at the Qatar General Retirement and Pension Authority, which included overseeing the management of hedge fund, private equity, real estate, fixed income, and equity portfolio investments. He has also worked for Qatar Energy and Dolphin Energy early in his career.

Yousuf serves as board member in leading local and international organisations including the World Alliance of International Financial Centers, the Qatar Financial Centre, the Qatar Finance and Business Academy, the Qatar Free Zones Authority, the Investment Promotion Agency, and Sidra Medicine.

    What is a Double Taxation Agreement?

    Double Taxation Agreements (DTAs) are treaties between two or more jurisdictions to avoid double taxation. They allow the contracting jurisdictions to share their taxation rights in relation to income earned by their respective residents. They also allow prevention of tax evasion and ensure that taxpayers in both jurisdictions are treated equally.

    Qatar’s DTAs

    Qatar has concluded more than 80 DTAs with most of its economic partners. While there are certain common features in these DTAs, each agreement remains a different and separate treaty with its own provisions. It is, therefore, necessary to check the respective agreement to ascertain the tax position and the benefits potentially available under the treaty.

    Treaty Benefits

    Tax benefits under DTA include non-taxation of non-resident companies with no permanent establishments in Qatar and reduced rates of (or exemption from) withholding tax on payments made to Qatari residents, provided certain conditions are met.
